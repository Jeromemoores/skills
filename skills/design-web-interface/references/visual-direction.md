# Visual direction and composition

## Start from content and context

Distinguish task-oriented applications, reading/editorial experiences, marketing and more experiential work. Choose emphasis, density and imagery to fit. Carbon's compact/productive and expressive typography illustrate this distinction; its font and exact tokens remain its own system. [Carbon typography](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/foundations/typography/overview)

Preserve an established brand unless changing it is part of the brief. A familiar font, purple palette, repeated component or conventional layout can be the correct choice. Avoid replacing them merely to prove originality. For a new direction, use subject matter and real content to choose a coherent visual language rather than applying an unrelated theme.

References are evidence for particular properties, not templates to clone. Annotate useful hierarchy, image role, grouping, density or rhythm and explain the fit. Check imagery/font availability and reuse terms before adopting assets. Prefer project-provided assets and compatible local choices when they meet the brief. A text-only reference index cannot establish an image's composition or a rendered page's appearance.

## Establish useful relationships

- **Hierarchy:** make reading order, primary information and important actions perceptible. Large typography is one option; contrast, position, grouping and whitespace also establish priority.
- **Type:** define roles for headings, body, labels and data. Check actual content, wrapping, readable measure, available weights and the supported writing systems. Do not demand a new display font for every project.
- **Spacing/alignment:** use a coherent rhythm and proximity to show relationships. Preserve enough density for comparison/scanning. A spacing scale is a project choice; no universal 8px rule follows from a particular design system.
- **Color:** assign meaningful roles and maintain adequate contrast for affected states. Color can express brand or state without coloring every label. Do not infer contrast ratios by eye.
- **Structure:** choose rows, tables, lists, narrative sections or cards for their information. Repeated records often benefit from consistency; unrelated content need not share one container treatment.
- **Imagery:** choose a useful message and focal point; account for crop, captions when useful, missing-media behavior and asset cost. A decorative hero is optional, not an automatic requirement.
- **Motion:** use it deliberately for feedback, continuity or a justified expressive moment. Keep interaction responsive and respect reduced-motion needs. No animation quota or mandatory library.

These are design criteria to apply to the brief, not standardized numerical quality tests. [Carbon spacing](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/foundations/spacing/overview) and [GOV.UK spacing](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/spacing/) demonstrate different coherent systems.

## Adapt and verify

Design for constrained widths and meaningful intermediate layouts. Preserve reading/action order, necessary controls and content. Reconsider columns, navigation, density and image crops. A dense comparison table may legitimately need two-dimensional treatment; do not turn it into oversized cards merely to remove all horizontal scrolling. [GOV.UK layout](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/layout/), [W3C reflow guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html)

Inspect real rendered pages at relevant widths and states. Check whether the direction survives actual content, long labels, failed media and focus/hover/disabled treatment. Describe the specific problem and expected benefit of a revision. Fix clipping by finding the layout cause; hiding overflow must not conceal required content or controls.

Check visible text fidelity, including punctuation, symbols, numbers and supported writing systems. Use the actual file/response encoding when reading or writing text. Garbled terminal output alone does not prove source corruption: compare decoded source and the browser render before correcting it. Repair encoding at its cause and preserve intended meaning; do not strip legitimate non-ASCII text to hide a defect.

Primary sources above inform the relationships and verification. A product design system's exact breakpoints, typefaces, scales, color choices and voice remain contextual conventions. Distinguish documented constraints from your own art direction.
